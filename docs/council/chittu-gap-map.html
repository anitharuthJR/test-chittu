The Gap Register

Ranked by what blocks launch, not what's easiest. CRITICAL blocks any real child using the product; HIGH blocks scale beyond the first market; MEDIUM blocks specific channels or markets.

GAP-01

Real children have never touched it

Critical

Today: Zero child users. Every design decision is adult inference — including the council's.

World-class meansA standing children's panel per launch culture — neurodivergent, disabled, cheap-phone, every accent — using each release candidate before adults sign off, feedback answered in writing, and "kids stopped talking to it" tracked as a safety metric (Seat 38's abstention made law).

Owners Seats 13 · 17 · 38 · 42 · Blocks GATE G5 · Phase 1
GAP-02

Classifiers are regex, not models

Critical

Today: Keyword matching catches "I want to die" and misses "everyone would be happier without me." No grooming-pattern detection. No adversarial validation.

World-class meansML classifiers on input and output: a crisis model validated against clinical scenario sets (Seat 12 signs), a grooming/exploitation detector built on Seat 14's threat corpus, red-team pass with zero criticals per release, and per-cohort parity proof so no accent or dialect is the one it fails on.

Owners Seats 12 · 14 · 18 · 19 · 44 · Blocks GATES G2 · G3 · Phase 1
GAP-03

Safety speaks only English

Critical

Today: A crisis disclosure typed in Tamil, Hindi or Telugu sails past every guard. The product is bilingual in charm, monolingual in protection.

World-class meansThe entire safety stack — crisis, PII, topic, spine triggers — ships per language, tested per language, before that language is enabled for chat. The rule: no language launches charming before it launches safe. Tamil is the proving ground; the per-language playbook it produces is the scaling asset.

Owners Seats 28 · 44 · 12 · Blocks GATES G2 · G3 per language · Phase 1→3
GAP-04

No voice — pre-literate children excluded

Critical

Today: Text-only. The 4–7 band — the youngest, and nearly all first-generation learners — cannot use their own product.

World-class meansVoice-first: child-speech ASR with accuracy floors per age and accent (adult-trained models fail children badly), transcribe-and-delete audio, no voiceprints, wake-word on-device, and latency a five-year-old will tolerate — squarely inside Arun's sub-700ms real-time stack competence.

Owners Seats 16 · 21 · 24 · 37 · Blocks GATE G5 for 4–7 band · Phase 2
GAP-05

Consent is mocked, and only Indian

Critical

Today: A demo button pretends to be DigiLocker. No COPPA methods, no GDPR member-state matrix, no revocation propagation to backend systems.

World-class meansThe consent engine as product: real DigiLocker/consent-manager integration for India, COPPA-approved methods for the US, proportional assurance for the UK, one tamper-evident consent ledger with per-jurisdiction adapters — under 90 seconds for a parent, producible on demand for a regulator.

Owners Seats 20 · 01 · 02 · 03 · 04 · 23 · Blocks GATE G1 · Phase 1 (India) → 3
GAP-06

Helplines are hardcoded

Critical

Today: Childline 1098 and KIRAN, hardcoded for India. A child in crisis anywhere else gets the wrong country's number.

World-class meansA verified helpline registry — per country, per language, re-verified quarterly like production infrastructure, with a named owner and an alert when any number changes. A wrong helpline is a P0 incident, not a content bug.

Owners Seats 12 · 22 · 32 · Blocks GATE G3 per market · Phase 1→3
GAP-07

Accessibility is absent

High

Today: No screen-reader optimisation, no captions plan for voice, no switch access, untested with disabled children.

World-class meansWCAG 2.2 AA as floor, visual equivalents for every audio moment, AAC-friendly modes, and disabled children on the testing panel — not proxies. The deaf child whose sibling raves about Chittu gets a first-class product.

Owners Seats 17 · 27 · Blocks GATE G5 · Phase 2
GAP-08

Untested on the ₹8,000 phone

High

Today: Built and reviewed on flagship hardware and good wifi — the exact demo Seat 37 refuses to watch.

World-class meansThe device floor as a release gate: shared low-cost Android on 3G, offline-tolerant degradation, tiny payloads, and a "shared family phone" mode where child profiles are properly separated on one device.

Owners Seats 24 · 37 · Blocks GATE G5 · Phase 2
GAP-09

Security & privacy program on paper only

High

Today: Client-side demo; no pen test, no DPIA, no breach drill, no named-humans access control, no CERT-In six-hour rehearsal.

World-class meansZero-trust backend, encryption everywhere, ISO 27001 → SOC 2 path, quarterly breach drills against both the DPDP 72-hour and CERT-In 6-hour clocks, a living DPIA that matches production, and an annual independent child-safety audit with published summary.

Owners Seats 30 · 31 · 43 · Blocks GATE G4 · Phase 1
GAP-10

Culture ships as translation

High

Today: One cultural voice with Tamil garnish. No cultural review boards, no per-market story canon, no festival/belief calibration.

World-class meansLocalisation as content architecture: cultural review board per market, stories and examples native to each child's world, and Seat 35's canon rule intact everywhere — the AI is a companion inside the story, never the author of the child's inner life.

Owners Seats 28 · 33 · 35 · Blocks each language launch · Phase 2→3
GAP-11

No store approval strategy

Medium

Today: A web demo. Apple Kids Category — famously wary of open generative chat for children — hasn't been engaged.

World-class meansKids Category compliance built-in (no third-party trackers, restricted SDKs), the pipeline-trace demo packaged as the reviewer's three-second answer, and safe-harbor certification (kidSAFE-class) pursued as a trust asset for stores, parents and enterprise buyers alike.

Owners Seats 10 · 31 · Blocks distribution · Phase 2
GAP-12

School channel unbuilt

Medium

Today: The product doesn't know when it's in a classroom — and thirty students on one teacher's rollout is a different legal universe (FERPA, school-consent rules, student-data DPAs).

World-class meansA distinct school mode: institutional consent flows where lawful, teacher dashboard, classroom-reality features tested in Seat 40's class of thirty with the wifi down, and DPAs schools can actually sign.

The Phase Plan

Each phase closes named gaps and exits through named gates. The sequence is deliberate: prove the safety machine in one language and one law first, then teach the machine to travel.

Phase 0 — The Skeleton

Done · Aug 2026

What exists: the doctrine (44 seats, 10 laws), the six-gate system, and a working prototype proving the architecture — consent-before-existence, scripted spine, bounded sandbox, output filter, health defaults, parent dashboard, hash-chained audit. The pitch asset: the pipeline trace — showing an investor or app-store reviewer exactly which layer answered each message.

Phase 1 — India, Properly

~2 quarters · exit gates G1–G5

One market, zero shortcuts: English + Tamil, full legal reality, real children. India is chosen because it is the hardest bar (under-18, DigiLocker VPC) — clear it and most of the world is architecture reuse. Closes GAP-01, 02, 05, 06, 09; opens 03.

Why this map is the pitch — note for the investor deck

Every kids' AI company will claim to be safe. Almost none can show a 44-seat doctrine, a six-gate release system, and a gap register that names its own weaknesses with owners and exit criteria — written before scale, not after the first incident.

The moat isn't the model. It's the machinery of trust around the model: the consent engine, the per-language safety playbook, the tamper-evident audit layer, the children's panels. Each is expensive to copy, compounds with every market, and is exactly the infrastructure Sakshi's Four A's were built to power. The gap map isn't an admission — it's the roadmap only a company that took children seriously from day zero could have written.
